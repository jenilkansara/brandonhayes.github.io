I study the granular mechanics of environmental flows — sand dunes, snow avalanches, and the way force travels through grains — in the Granular Flow Laboratory led by Nathalie Vriend.

I spent my PhD studying small-scale multiphase flows in additive manufacturing and microfluidics in CU Boulder's MACLab, with an emphasis on bubble physics. Now my focus is large-scale environmental flows, using a mix of experimental and computational methods — quantitative photoelasticity, immersion rheometry, and DEM–CFD simulation.

Outside the lab, I can likely be found swimming, hiking, exploring, and biking around in the mountains near Boulder.

Research interests

Granular mechanics of environmental flows

Dry and wet granular mechanics in environmental flows — sand dunes, snow avalanches, and the link between particle-scale interactions and bulk frictional behavior.

Photoelastic force chains

Quantitative photoelastic analysis of forces and the stress tensor in soft granular media, including a large-scale immersion-based photoelastic rheometer.

Multi-material 3D printing

Inkjet liquid–solid co-printing of complex, highly-viscous and viscoelastic fluids for 3D micro/mesofluidic devices.

Flash-boiling micro-actuation

Thermal bubble-driven micro-pumps for microfluidics, micro-robotics, and droplet ejection — from nucleation physics to device fabrication.

Methods span experiment and computation: quantitative photoelasticity, immersion rheometry, high-speed/stroboscopic imaging, and DEM, DEM–CFD, and multiphase simulation (OpenFOAM, FLOW-3D, preCICE). Author of APEGS, the first photoelastic grain solver to extend analysis from circular disks to arbitrarily-shaped particles.
